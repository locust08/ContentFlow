1. PROBLEM STATEMENT 
1.1. BACKGROUND OF THE PROBLEM 
Small businesses use websites to manage and present digital content such as business 
information, services, products, contact details, and blog articles (Campbell, 2018; 
SBDCNet, 2025). Many small business owners already have websites built using 
platforms such as WordPress, Joomla, Drupal, or similar systems (Godreau, 2025; 
Iqbal et al., 2020), but the content still needs to be maintained after the website is 
created (SBDCNet, 2025). This includes keeping pages active (SBDCNet, 2025), 
adding basic SEO such  as meta title and meta description (Google Search Central, 
2025), creating blog content for long-term visibility (Riserbato, 2026), and setting up 
tracking tools such as GTM and GA4 to support campaign measurement and website 
performance tracking (Google for Developers, 2026). 
1.2. PROBLEM IDENTIFICATION 
The main problem is that small business owners often struggle to manage and improve 
their existing website content in a simple and affordable way (SBDCNet, 2025; 
Restrepo-Morales et al., 2024). Creating a new website can be expensive (Masoner, 
2026), while maintaining content, visibility, and tracking setup also requires time, cost, 
and regular effort (SBDCNet, 2025; Google Search Central, 2025; Google for 
Developers, 2026). Many small businesses do not have their own digital team 
(Sutherland et al., 2025), so their websites may become inactive, outdated, not properly 
prepared for search visibility (SBDCNet, 2025; Google Search Central, 2025), or not 
ready to measure campaign performance through tools such as GTM and GA4 (Google 
for Developers, 2026).

1.3. IMPORTANCE OF THE PROBLEM 
This problem affects small business owners who rely on their websites to promote their 
business, share information, and attract potential customers (Godreau, 2025; 
SBDCNet, 2025). If website content, basic SEO, and tracking setup are not managed 
properly, the business may lose online visibility, customer trust, potential enquiries, 
and useful performance data (Godreau, 2025; Google for Developers, 2026; Google 
Search Central, 2025). Therefore, a solution is needed to help small businesses manage 
existing website content, support basic search visibility, and prepare their websites for 
measurement more efficiently (Google for Developers, 2026; Google Search Central, 
2025; SBDCNet, 2025). This aligns with CLO1 because the project focuses on 
designing a solution to a problem in managing digital content. 
2. OBJECTIVES 
2.1. GENERAL OBJECTIVE 
To design and develop SiteTarik, a digital content management platform that helps 
Malaysian small business owners manage their existing website content through 
website duplication, basic SEO, blog content support, tracking setup, and WhatsApp -
based delivery. 
2.2. SPECIFIC OBJECTIVES (SMART) 
The specific objectives are developed based on the SMART approach to ensure they 
are clear, measurable, and achievable (University of California, n.d.). 
1. To design and develop a web platform that allows users to submit their business 
details, website URL, selected package, and WhatsApp consent by the end of 
Week 18. 
2. To design a mobile application prototype that allows project or submission 
information to be viewed clearly by the end of Week 18. 
3. To develop a structured data storage method using Stripe metadata to store 
customer details, website information, package selection, blog brief details, and 
delivery status by the end of Week 18. 
4. To test the SiteTarik prototype with at least 10 small business clients and collect 
feedback on usability, package understanding, form completion, and overall 
experience before the final presentation in Week 18.

3. TARGET USERS AND USE CASES 
3.1. TARGET USERS 
3.1.1. Small Business Owners with Existing Websites  
Small business owners are the main users of SiteTarik. They already have an 
existing website and need a simple way to manage website content, basic SEO, 
blog content, and tracking setup without depending fully on expensive or 
manual services. 
3.1.2. SiteTarik Administrators 
SiteTarik administrators are responsible for reviewing customer submissions, 
checking the selected package, managing the website duplication or blog 
content process, and sending the final delivery through WhatsApp. 
3.1.3. Client Testers 
Client testers are small business owners who will test the SiteTarik prototype. 
Their feedback will be used to evaluate usability, package understanding, form 
completion, and overall experience. 
3.2. USE CASE SCENARIOS  
3.2.1. Use Case 1: Small Business Owner Selects Core Reborn 
The user submits their business details, website URL, package selection, and 
WhatsApp consent. The system records the submission and guides the user to 
payment. After payment is completed, the user receives confirmation and waits 
for the website delivery through WhatsApp. The delivered website will include 
one-to-one duplication, basic SEO, and tracking setup where applicable. 
3.2.2. Use Case 2: Small Business Owner Selects SEO Enhancement 
The user submits the main form and completes payment. The system opens the 
Blog Brief form for the user to provide blog content details. The submitted 
information is then stored for blog content planning and delivery. The delivered 
website will include basic SEO, tracking setup, and blog content support.

5. SYSTEM OVERVIEW / ARCHITECTURE 
5.1. SYSTEM OVERVIEW 
SiteTarik works as a simple web and mobile -based system for small business owners 
with existing websites. The user fills in the main form, selects either Core Reborn or 
SEO Enhancement, completes payment, and receives updates through WhatsApp. For 
Core Reborn, the system supports one-to-one website duplication with basic SEO. For 
SEO Enhancement, the system also collects blog details through the Blog Brief form 
and supports blog content planning for long-term visibility. 
5.2. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE 
SiteTarik consists of six main components: frontend, backend, data storage, external 
tools and APIs, hosting platform, and notification system. 
5.2.1. Frontend 
The frontend will be built using Next.js for the web platform. It will include the 
landing page, customer form, package selection, payment flow, Blog Brief page, 
and Thank You page. A simple mobile application prototype will also be 
included to display project or submission information clearly. 
5.2.2. Backend 
The backend will handle form submission, package selection, payment flow, 
Blog Brief submission, and order confirmation. It will also help manage the 
flow between the landing page, Stripe payment, Blog Brief page, Thank You 
page, and WhatsApp delivery. 
5.2.3. Data Storage 
The prototype will use Stripe metadata as a simple storage method for customer 
details, website information, package selection, blog brief details, and delivery 
status. This is suitable for the current project scope because SiteTarik is 
designed as a simple and low-cost prototype.

Stripe will be used for payment, WhatsApp will be used for chatbot guidance 
and delivery updates, and Cloudflare will be used to host the final website

GTM and GA4 may be used to track user actions for evaluation (Google Search

Reborn, the admin and client will receive email notifications after payment is 
completed. For SEO Enhancement, the admin and client will receive email

customer, payment, and blog brief details.

6. METHODOLOGY 
6.1. DEVELOPMENT APPROACH 
This project will use the Spiral development approach. This approach is suitable for 
SiteTarik because the system has several connected parts, including the web platform, 
mobile application prototype, Stripe payment flow, Stripe metadata storage, Blog Brief 
form, website duplication, Cloudflare hosting, and WhatsApp-based delivery (Boehm 
& Hansen, 2001). 
The Spiral approach allows each part of the system to be planned, developed, tested, 
and improved in stages (Boehm, 1988). This is important because possible issues such 
as form errors, payment problems, unclear package selection, incomplete blog details, 
and website duplication accuracy can be identified early (Boehm, 1988; Boehm & 
Hansen, 2001). 
Overall, the Spiral approach is chosen because it supports gradual development, risk 
checking, and continuous improvement (Boehm, 1988; Boehm & Hansen, 2001). This 
aligns with CLO1 because the project focuses on designing a practical solution for 
managing digital content for small businesses. 
6.2. DEVELOPMENT PHASES 
The development of this project is divided into five main phases: planning, design, 
development, testing, and evaluation.  
6.2.1. Planning Phase 
The planning phase will identify the project problem, objectives, target users, 
system scope, and main requirements. This phase ensures that SiteTarik is 
focused on helping small businesses manage existing website content, basic 
SEO, blog content support, basic GTM and GA4 setup, and WhatsApp -based 
delivery. 
6.2.2. Design Phase 
The design phase will prepare the system flow, web platform layout, mobile 
application prototype, form structure, payment flow, data storage plan, and user 
journey. This phase ensures that the system is simple, clear, and suitable for 
small business users.

6.2.3. Development Phase 
The development phase will build the main SiteTarik prototype. This includes 
the landing page, customer form, package selection, Stripe payment flow, Blog 
Brief form, Thank You page, Stripe metadata storage, Gmail notification, 
WhatsApp chatbot guidance, and Cloudflare hosting setup. 
6.2.4. Testing Phase 
The testing phase will check whether the main functions work properly. This 
includes testing the form submission, payment flow, Blog Brief form, data 
storage, Gmail notification, website duplication process, basic SEO support, 
basic GTM and GA4 setup, and WhatsApp delivery flow. 
6.2.5. Evaluation Phase 
The evaluation phase will test the prototype with at least 10 small business 
clients. Their feedback will be collected to evaluate usability, package 
understanding, form completion, and overall experience before the final 
presentation. 
Overall, these phases support gradual development and continuous improvement 
throughout the project.

9. CONCLUSION / JUSTIFICATION 
9.1. SUMMARY OF PROPOSAL 
SiteTarik is proposed as a digital content management platform for Malaysian small 
business owners who already have existing websites. The main problem is that many 
small businesses struggle to maintain website content, basic SEO, blog content 
(SBDCNet, 2025; Riserbato, 2026), and tracking setup because it requires time, cost, 
and regular effort (Restrepo -Morales et al., 2024). SiteTarik solves this problem by 
providing a simple web and mobile -based system that supports website duplication, 
basic SEO, blog cont ent planning, basic GTM and GA4 setup, payment flow, data 
storage using Stripe metadata (Stripe, n.d.), and WhatsApp-based delivery. 
9.2. PROJECT JUSTIFICATION 
This project is worth developing because it helps small business owners manage their 
existing website content in a simpler and more affordable way. The system supports 
digital content management by collecting customer details, storing submission 
information, supporting basic SEO, collecting blog content details, setting up basic 
GTM and GA4 tracking, and helping users receive website delivery through 
WhatsApp. It also supports the project objectives by including a web platform, mobile 
application prototype, data storage, and client testing with at least 10 small business 
clients. This aligns with CLO1 because SiteTarik is designed as a practical solution to 
a real problem in managing digital content.
